# orrery × MiniMax H3

How orrery writes MiniMax H3's prompts: you write a screenplay (shots, camera, voices, sound, a
CAST of references), orrery compiles it to H3's official format for every mode, and a reel chains
clips into a film that can branch and go on forever. Built since 2026-09-23; the reel's words are
DSL 2.0's (2026-10-03).
Sources: the official H3 prompting guides (`docs/VIDEO_PROMPT_WRITING_GUIDE_base_en.md`,
`..._ref_en.md` in `MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3`), the official `h3-prompt-writing` skill
(`MiniMax-AI/MiniMax-H3/skills`), and lessons from Pyro's H3 LoRA campaign.

## What H3 actually consumes

An H3 prompt is not a string. It is a document with a contract:

- **Base modes** (T2VA, I2VA, FL2VA, L2VA): three fields in fixed order,
  `integrated_multimodal_description`, `overall_soundscape`,
  `non_diegetic_music`. Keyframe modes add a position-sensitive alignment
  instruction as the first line, carrying the effective duration as `S.SS`
  and the index of the final shot.
- **Full-reference mode** (Ref2VA): six sections (`subject_definitions`,
  `summary`, `retention_analysis`, `detailed_description`,
  `overall_soundscape`, `non_diegetic_music`) with `<Subject N>`,
  `<Picture N>`, `<Video N>`, `<Audio N>` labels whose meaning must stay stable
  across all sections.
- **Timeline:** `[Shot 1]` has no timestamp; later shots start with
  `At MM:SS.mmm,` and a cut verb. Timestamps increase strictly and stay inside
  the duration (4–15 s).
- **Voices:** global speaker IDs `(S1)`, `(S2)`, assigned in the order of the
  first vocal event and stable across shots. Dialogue is `<d>[Language] …</d>`
  (`<d>` is a real tokenizer special token), verbatim, never translated.
  Voiceovers use an exact phrase plus a "lips remain closed" clause;
  `<scenetrans>` and `<cutoff>` mark speech across cuts and truncated speech.
- **Camera:** a closed vocabulary (push in/pull out, zoom, pan, truck, tilt,
  pedestal, arc, tracking, static, shake, POV, roll) plus optional amplitude
  (small/large) and speed (slow/fast), written as a natural English sentence.
- **Sound:** `overall_soundscape` is 1–4 sentences of ambience and physical
  sounds; `non_diegetic_music` describes instrumentation, tempo and dynamics,
  never mood words. `N/A` has different rules in the two fields.
- **Weights do nothing:** ComfyUI's H3 tokenizer runs with
  `disable_weights=True`, so `(word:1.2)` is inert. orrery weights are sampling
  weights only and must never be emitted as attention syntax for H3.

MiniMax states that its hosted rewriter, **H3-Context-IR**, is critical to
output quality and did not open-source it. The public substitute is the
`h3-prompt-writing` skill: an LLM reads the guide and rewrites. Community tools
(Fantastic H3 Prompt Builder) fill templates by hand with live checking.

## The pattern: screenplay in, contract out

orrery treats H3 as a compiler target. One scene model, many writers (the
Pandoc pattern).

```
screenplay template ──expand (seeded, records picks)──▶ scene model ──writer──▶ H3 prompt
                                                           │                    ▲
                                                           └──────lint──────────┘
```

### 1. Front end: a screenplay-shaped template

Video is a screenplay, so the surface looks like one (after the Fountain
plaintext screenplay format). Every orrery construct (`__lib__`, `{a|b}`,
`$bind`, `>`) works inside any slot.

```
@h3 text 16:9
style: live-action, cinematic
$hero = __subjects/animals__

SHOT 5s: push in, small, slow
A {misty|frozen} forest at dawn. $hero steps onto a fallen log, breath visible.
NARRATOR (calm low voice, off-screen): The forest remembers.
SFX: branches creak under frost; soft footfalls on snow

SHOT 3s: cut, static
A close-up of $hero's eyes glowing amber as snow drifts past.
SFX: wind hisses through the trees

MUSIC: sparse __instrument__ at a slow tempo, fading out
```

| Line | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `@h3 <mode> [ratio] [0.6MP] [full]` | target and mode: `text`, `image` (the clip starts on a picture), `first-last`, `last` (it ends on one) or `references` (a CAST of pictures, videos, voices and RefMods); MiniMax's own names (`t2va`, `i2va`, `fl2va`, `l2va`, `ref2va`) work too. `0.6MP` sets the canvas area, so the node's `width`/`height` are that many megapixels in the ratio's shape (square without one) and its `megapixels` output carries it; the prompt is `lite` (subject definitions over three fields) unless `full` asks for the guide's full format (1c) |
| `style: …` | style and look; opens `[Shot 1]` as its own sentence |
| `SHOT <n>s: [transition,] camera` | a shot with its duration (`SHOT 5s \| …`, the older spelling, still works); transition `cut` (default), `dissolve`, `fade`, `wipe`; camera from the official vocabulary, or `__camera__` |
| prose lines | the visible action of the shot |
| `@NAME (voice[, off-screen\|voiceover]): text` | a vocal event (a CAST member as `@NAME`, anyone else by a name in capitals); text may start with `[German]` etc., default `[English]` |
| `SFX: a; b; c` | physical and ambient sound, one sentence per line (“A, B, and C.”), collected into `overall_soundscape`; `SFX: silence` means `N/A`; without `SFX:` lines the soundscape asks for natural foley and ambience that fit the scene |
| `MUSIC: …` | audience-only score; absent means `N/A` |

Total duration is the sum of shot durations. Keyframes are referenced in prose
as `<Picture 1>`, `<Picture 2>`.

A template without `SHOT` lines, a Krea prompt for instance, compiles as one
5 s shot (H3's default length): its prose becomes Shot 1, `SFX:` lines its
sound; `: x8 w… h…` and `> …` lines are left out.

### 1b. The cast and references (`@h3 references`)

References get names once, in a `CAST` block before the first `SHOT`; shots,
voices and the `summary:` line mention them as `@NAME`. In the editor `@` lists
the cast, and every member shows in brass. (Fountain forces a character the same
way; a bare `MAYA` works too.)

```
@h3 references 16:9 full
style: realistic multi-camera sitcom style with warm indoor lighting
summary: The target video shows @MAYA eating a cookie in @CAFE. @LEO enters with @DOG.

CAST
@CAFE (image 1): the coffee-shop environment, featuring an exposed brick wall and an orange sofa
@DOG (image 2, image 3): the fluffy white Samoyed, with thick white fur and a curved tail
@MAYA (video 1): the young blonde woman, with long blonde hair and a light-pink shirt
voice: audio 1, containing a spoken English vocal layer
keep: fully_preserved - her identity, long hair, and light-pink shirt are retained.

SHOT 3s: from image 5
A medium shot establishes @CAFE. @MAYA sits on the sofa as @DOG lunges for her cookie.
@MAYA (light annoyance): Hey! Watch your dog!
```

| Line | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `@NAME (sources): head, details` | a member. Sources: `image N`, `image NAME` (a picture of the gallery by name, which Orrery Refs loads itself: see [orrery-refs.md](orrery-refs.md#pictures-by-name)), `video N`, `video N + audio` (its soundtrack is wired), `refmod NAME` and `always` (1e). A scene's own CAST replaces a member for that clip. `@name(0.8)` stays the LoRA shortcut for every name that is not a member's. Later mentions use the head noun: the part before the first comma, cut before `with`, `wearing`, `in`, `who`, `that`, `holding`, `carrying`, `facing` or `dressed` |
| `voice: audio N` / `voice: video N audio` | after a member: the voice timbre it speaks with; an optional `, note` follows |
| `keep: marker - reason`, `keep: face, outfit` | the retention line (`fully_preserved`, `partially_preserved`, `attribute_transfer`, `weak_reference`, or `full`/`partial`/`transfer`/`weak`), or a macro (`all`, `face`, `hair`, `body`, `outfit`, `style`, `place`, `loose`); any `keep:` brings a retention block, in `lite` too (1c) |
| `summary: …` | the summary paragraph of `references`; the `[task + type]` prefix is computed |
| `SHOT …: from image N (shows)` / `to image N` | a frame anchor: `<Picture N>` as the shot's first or last frame |
| `SHOT …: after video N` | the shot continues `<Video N>` from its final frame (`[video continuation]`); wire the video to continue (a Load Video, for instance) into that ref_video |
| `--directions--` | a slot the language model writes when the node runs, in any mode; CAST names in it arrive as labels. See the README |
| `[image N]`, `[video N]`, `[audio N]`, `[video N audio]` | a slot mentioned in prose; `<Image N>`, as H3's own prompts write it, is `[image N]`. A slot named in the text goes to H3 without a CAST (`a woman who looks like <Image 1>`) |

With `full`, the writer emits the six full-reference sections, leaving out any that would be
empty (H3 copes): `subject_definitions` and `retention_analysis` without a CAST,
frame anchors, voices or continuation, `summary` without a `summary:` line.
Labels follow the Reference to
Video node: `<Picture i>` and `<Video k>` by slot, `<Audio j>` first for each
video whose soundtrack is wired (in video order), then standalone audio. Speaker
IDs `(Sx)` follow the order of first vocal events; the first mention of a member
in the video carries its description, the first mention in a shot where it speaks
carries its ID. The task types come from the roles: frame anchors are keyframe
completion, subjects are reference generation, `voice:` is audio reference.
Lint checks the summary, slot limits, members without references, voices that
never speak, and the 350–500 word range of `detailed_description`.

Put **Orrery Refs** between your images and the Reference to Video node, and
`(image N)` keeps your numbering while each clip gets only its own images,
labelled from `<Picture 1>`: see the [Orrery Refs tutorial](orrery-refs.md).

In the other modes a cast needs no sources: names expand to their description on
first mention and to the head afterwards. That is also how RefMods bind to a
prompt (1e).

### 1c. Lite and full (`@h3 <mode> [ratio] full`)

The header picks only what orrery writes for H3, not your screenplay. By default it writes `lite`,
the short format most people write by hand for the Reference to Video node; `full` writes the full
reference format of MiniMax's prompt guide. (`lite` may still be written; it is the default.) The
same screenplay both ways:

```
@h3 references 16:9          ← add full here for the second version
style: live-action, cinematic
CAST
@KEEPER (image 1): an old lighthouse keeper in a yellow oilskin coat
SHOT 5s: push in, slow
@KEEPER climbs the iron stairs with a lantern.
SFX: boots ring on iron steps
```

`lite`, the default:

```
<Subject 1> = an old lighthouse keeper of <Picture 1> in a yellow oilskin coat

integrated_multimodal_description: [Shot 1] Live-action, cinematic. <Subject 1> climbs the iron stairs with a lantern. The camera pushes in at slow speed.

overall_soundscape: Boots ring on iron steps.

non_diegetic_music: N/A
```

`full`:

```
subject_definitions:
<Subject 1> is an old lighthouse keeper in <Picture 1>, in a yellow oilskin coat.

retention_analysis:
<Subject 1> (appears in [Shot 1]): fully_preserved - the defined appearance of the old lighthouse keeper is retained.

detailed_description:
The target video uses a live-action, cinematic style.
[Shot 1] <Subject 1>, an old lighthouse keeper in a yellow oilskin coat, climbs the iron stairs with a lantern. The camera pushes in at slow speed.

overall_soundscape:
Boots ring on iron steps.

non_diegetic_music:
N/A
```

So `lite` is one `<Subject N> = …` line per cast member, then three fields in which every cast
member is only its label: no summary, the subject not described again in the prose. It works in
every mode, and text-only cast members (no image) are fine. A `voice:` adds
`<Audio j> = the voice of <Subject N>`; `image`, `first-last` and `last` keep their alignment line
after the definitions. `summary:` and the 350–500 words the guide asks for belong to `full`: a summary in
`lite` warns.

**Retention.** A `keep:` line under a cast member puts a `retention_analysis` block into the
prompt in every format and mode (in `full` references it is always there): every member, the shots it
appears in, a marker and a reason. Write the marker and the reason yourself, or one of the macros:

| `keep:` | writes |
|---|---|
| `all` | `fully_preserved` - retained as defined, in every detail |
| `face`, `hair`, `body`, `outfit`, combined (`face, outfit`) | `partially_preserved` - only those parts are retained, everything else follows the prompt |
| `style` | `attribute_transfer` - only its style (colours, materials, finish) carries over |
| `place` (or `location`, `background`) | `fully_preserved` - retained as a place: its layout, surfaces and light |
| `loose` | `weak_reference` - a loose reference whose details may change |
| `partially_preserved - only her face and the scar` | that marker and your reason (`full`, `partial`, `transfer`, `weak` too) |

Runs from before lite was the default come back from History and Gallery with `full` on their
`@h3` line, so they write the prompt they wrote then. Which format H3 follows better has not been
compared yet; the night watch reel uses `lite`.

### 1d. Reels (`SCENE`)

A reel is one screenplay for a chain of clips, each continuing the one before:
with orrery's own Orrery Continue / Orrery Film ([comfyui.md](comfyui.md)) or
with H3 Motion Context. Everything before the first `SCENE` (header, style,
`CAST`, bindings, `LORA:`, `context:`) holds for every clip; each `SCENE` holds
the shots of one clip. Clips count from 1 wherever you read or write them; the
node's `segment` widget counts from 0 (0 is clip 1).

| Line | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `SCENE [title] [×N \| forever] [(test)]` | starts a clip. The title names it for `CUT TO:`, `AFTER:` and `$x["title"]`; `×4` plays it four times, `forever` until you stop; `(test)`: see *Test scenes* below |
| `END ON: …` | inside a scene: its closing sentence ("The shot ends as …") and the opening one of the clip after it ("The shot opens as …"); `IF $take is 2: END ON: …` holds when the condition does (#347) |
| `START WITH: …` | this scene's own opening sentence, in place of the `END ON:` before it (the first clip and a test scene too) |
| `AFTER: 2` | continue scene 2's last clip (by title, or its number from 1) instead of the clip before; see *Branches* below |
| `AFTER: the input video` | continue the video wired into the Orrery Prompt; see *The input video* below |
| `CUT TO: the stairs ×2 (30%)` | at a scene's end: jump there instead of going on; `×N` N times, `(30%)` that often; see below |
| `IF $w is a storm: CUT TO: shelter` | a jump on what this clip rolled: the condition reads the clip's own bindings (their tags, properties, value) |
| `REMEMBER: first frame as @GIRL` | frames of this clip, kept for the clips after it; see *Anchors* below |
| `REMEMBER: every 10th frame as refmod NAME` | in any mode: a RefMod made of those frames, wherever a CAST member names `refmod NAME` (1e) |
| `LORA: <lora:name:0.8>` | put on the model that passes through the Orrery Prompt, its model strength (names resolve like LoraManager's, by file name or path): before the first `SCENE` for every clip, inside one for that clip only; `<lora:name:0.5,1.0>` or `0-1;0.1` makes it a sweep that Roll runs once per strength, `:solo` and `:test` as in [LoRA sweeps](wildcard-manager.md) |
| `context: 22` | the frames each clip continues from (default 22; Orrery Continue pins 22 and warns about another number, Motion Context also takes 5, 39, 56 and 0 for none): in every clip that continues one they are added to Shot 1 and to `length` |
| `MUSIC:` | before the first `SCENE` for all, inside one for that clip |
| `style: …` | inside a scene: that clip's style, in place of the head's (an empty room in a "TikTok reel" invites someone in front of the camera) |
| `: grid __cases__` | a grid in a reel: Roll renders every cell on the same clip (the segment holds still), so `SET: __cases__` in a scene renders all its cases at once |

The words of earlier orrery still work, with the same dice: `CHUNK` (SCENE),
`repeat N` / `repeat forever`, `HANDOFF:` (END ON:), `GOTO:` (CUT TO:),
`SEND: … to image N [for segment N]` (REMEMBER, its segments from 0), `$x~N`
(`$x[-N]`), `? $x[a]:` (IF) and `global` (always).

Everything before the first `SCENE` is the **world**: it rolls once per seed,
so its `$bindings` hold across clips. Every **clip** (repeats counted) rolls
its scene with its own seed, derived from the node's seed and the clip's
number: a repeated scene varies, and every clip is reproducible.
`$x[-1]` is binding `x` as it was one clip ago (`$x[-2]` two), and
`$x["the stairs"]` as it was the last time that scene played (`.field` after
either reads a property). Both are recomputed rather than remembered, so there
is no history limit; before the first clip, or for a scene that has not played
yet, they are the value this clip rolls (with a warning). Picks are recorded
for the world, the clip and its `END ON:` lines.

**The past is what was rendered** (#261). With Orrery Film, each take keeps
what its clip rolled: every binding (with its fields) and its `END ON:`. A
clip after it reads its past from the takes on the film's path: the scene each
clip played (a `CUT TO:` that hangs on a roll goes where the film went), the
head's bindings, `$x[-1]`, `$x["the stairs"]` and the `END ON:` it opens with,
as they were rendered, though a library, a rating or an earlier scene's text
changed since; the lint says when a head binding stays as rendered where it
would roll otherwise now. What has no take yet rolls as before, and so does
everything without Orrery Film (the CLI, the golden corpus). To change the past
on purpose, render the earlier clip again: the clips after the new take follow it.

The node counts: the Prompt tab's **Next clip** (a hidden `segment` input that
steps on after each run unless **Hold** keeps it) is the clip to write;
Orrery Continue reads it from the picks. A Run count of N plays N clips; Run
(Instant) plays a `forever` reel (or one that loops with `CUT TO:`) until you
stop it; past the last clip of a finite reel nothing downstream runs. The CLI
prints every clip (the first six of a forever loop), or one with `--segment N`.

**CUT TO: loops and stories.** A scene's `CUT TO:` lines are tried when it has
played (after its last repeat), in the order written: the first that holds and
has jumps left wins, and when none does the reel goes on to the next scene. A
jump opens with the `END ON:` of the clip before it, as any clip does.

```
SCENE the stairs
SHOT 4s: tracking
The keeper climbs the stairs.
END ON: the keeper reaches the lamp room
SCENE the lamp
$w = {a storm|a calm night|fog}
SHOT 6s: push in
The keeper lights the lamp in $w.
END ON: the beam sweeps the sea
IF $w is a storm: CUT TO: the stairs ×3
SCENE the ship
…
```

Every storm sends the keeper up the stairs again, three times at most, and
then the ship comes in. `CUT TO: the stairs (30%)` does the same with odds of
its own, without a binding: it rolls each time the cut is tried, on dice that
move no pick of the clip. A condition or a chance makes the path depend on the
seed: seed 0 may play eight clips and seed 5 six. orrery walks the reel clip by
clip, so the node, the Test tab, the CLI and the writers all follow the same
path, and the app asks for it at the node's seed: a scene's divider names the
next clip it plays, its hover lists them all (`clips 2, 4, 6 · 3 × 4 s`), its 📊
shows where and how often it plays and what leads to it, and the timeline and
the cells follow it. A `CUT TO:` without `×N` loops for good (Run (Instant) plays it until
you stop); a scene that every jump passes by is flagged. The preset
`@fashion/infinite_cakewalk` is an endless show that rolls who walks next and
cuts to the scene that dresses them; `@loops/infinite_backrooms`,
`@loops/dice_dungeon`, `@loops/the_relay`, `@loops/evolution` and
`@loops/endless_kitchen` are more ([presets.md](presets.md)).

**Branches: `AFTER:`.** A scene with `AFTER: 2` continues scene 2's last clip,
not the clip before it in the template. So many scenes can branch off one
clip, each with its own `SET:`, and one Roll renders them all:

```
SCENE case A
AFTER: 2
SET: image_1(0), @JINX(0)
SHOT 7s: static
@JINX walks back into the room.

SCENE case B
AFTER: 2
SET: @JINX(0.1, images)
SHOT 7s: static
@JINX walks back into the room.
```

Both cases open on scene 2's `END ON:`, and Orrery Continue pins the tail of
scene 2's clip for both. `$x[-1]` and what was remembered follow the branch, so
case A's `REMEMBER:` lines stay out of case B. Orrery Film keeps every branch's
take (rendering case A again keeps case B); its joined film strings them
together in order, which reads as a film only along one path. A scene that
repeats continues itself from its second time on.

**Test scenes: `(test)`.** `SCENE the forest (test)` is rendered and kept like
any scene, but Orrery Film leaves it out when it joins the film, and it starts
afresh: no pinned frames, no opening sentence, no context in its length (with
`AFTER:` it continues that scene instead). What a test scene remembers reaches
the scenes after it, so a template can open with a few test scenes (five
biomes, say) whose frames become the pictures and RefMods of the film:

```
SCENE the forest (test)
SHOT 5s: slow pan
A misty pine forest at dawn, no people.
REMEMBER: every 10th frame as refmod forest_today

SCENE the walk
SHOT 7s: tracking
@HIKER walks through @FOREST.
```

The scene after test scenes continues the last clip that is in the film (or
starts afresh when there is none): the picture follows the film, the memory
follows the clips in order.

**A clip whose clip before was never made** starts afresh too (#415), by the
same rules: with Orrery Continue, rolling scene 5 before scenes 2 to 4 have a
clip gives it no pinned frames, no opening sentence from the `END ON:` before
it and no context in its length, and what the missing clips would have sent
stays out (what the film's clips sent still reaches it). The film keeps it at
its place and cuts to it; the clips after it continue it, and a clip made in the
gap later continues the one before the gap.

**The input video.** A video wired into the Orrery Prompt's `video` input
(Load Video) is the reel's scene before its first: the head speaks for it. An
`END ON:` in the head says how the video ends, and a `REMEMBER:` in the head
keeps the video's frames for every clip, as a scene's `REMEMBER:` does for the
clips after it. A scene continues the video only when it says so:

```
@h3 text 16:9
END ON: the camera holds on the empty kitchen
REMEMBER: frame at 2s as @COOK

SCENE the return
AFTER: the input video
SHOT 8s: static
@COOK walks back into the kitchen.
```

The return opens on the head's `END ON:`, and Orrery Continue pins the video's
last 22 frames and their sound, fitted to the header's canvas (it needs the H3
video and audio VAEs in its `vae` and `audio_vae`). Without `AFTER: the input
video` a reel starts afresh as before, and a later scene can say it too, to
branch off the video again. The Orrery Prompt keeps the video at 24 fps beside
the reel's clips (`orrery_input.mp4` in the chain folder), so its frames count
as a clip's do; Orrery Film leaves it out of the film. The example workflow
`orrery_h3_reel_input_video.json` plays a reel from a video.

**Anchors: `REMEMBER:`.** A reel drifts: each clip only sees the one before it.
`REMEMBER:` keeps frames of a scene's clip for the clips after it, as a picture
of a CAST member, a numbered picture, or a RefMod:

```
@h3 references 16:9 lite
CAST
@GIRL: the young woman, in a pink tracksuit
SCENE the first pose
SHOT 7s: push in, small, slow
@GIRL stretches on a mat.
REMEMBER: first frame as @GIRL
SCENE the walk
SHOT 5s: static
@GIRL walks to the window.
```

| Line | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `REMEMBER: first frame as @GIRL` | that frame becomes GIRL's picture: the first `image N` her CAST lines give her, or a free slot her CAST lines are given (`@GIRL (image 1): …` from then on), so no number ties the two together |
| `REMEMBER: frames 0, 50 as @GIRL` | a picture of hers per frame listed: her CAST's first pictures in order, then free slots (a range in the list stays one picture, a batch) |
| `REMEMBER: frame at 1s as image 3` | a numbered picture of the CAST; frames by word (`first frame`, `last frame`), by second (`frame at 1s`, `frames at 1s, 2s`, `1s-2s`) or by number (`frame 30`, `frames 2, 5, 34-46`, `-1` the last, `-24--1` its last second) |
| `REMEMBER: every 10th frame as refmod jinx_today` | a RefMod made of every 10th frame of the clip (`every 5th frame of 1s-4s`: of a range), see 1e |
| `… in clips 4+`, `… in clips 2-5`, `… in clips 4, 6, 7` | only those clips (from 1); several lines can fill one picture in turns |
| `… until the return` | until that scene first plays |

Frames count from 0 in the clip as the reel keeps it (Orrery Film, or H3
Motion Context's Chain Video: the pinned frames trimmed off), at 24 fps; a
negative frame counts from the clip's end; several frames as one image or
RefMod become one batch (as a member, one picture each). A scene that repeats remembers from the first time it plays. The
picture exists from the clip after that one, or only in the clips its `in
clips`/`until` allow; elsewhere orrery leaves it out of the clip (CAST sources
and frame anchors). Where two lines fill one picture in the same clip, the one
remembered last takes over (lint says so). That re-anchors a picture mid-reel:

```
SCENE the old look ×4
REMEMBER: first frame as image 3 in clips 2-5
…
SCENE the new look
REMEMBER: last frame as image 3 in clips 6+
```

Orrery Refs fetches the frames from the reel's folder (its chain, which orrery names
after the reel: see [comfyui.md](comfyui.md)), so the Orrery Prompt's picks go into an Orrery Refs. A picture wired
into a remembered `image_N` stands in until the frames exist, and the frames
replace it from then on: a reel can start from a reference picture and go on
with a frame of the video (the console says which picture a clip gets). Its
outputs are the images the prompt names, in `<Picture N>` order, then every
other remembered picture that exists and that no CAST gives a member (a
member's picture goes only to the clips where a member has it): with only
`REMEMBER: frame 0 as image 1` and no CAST, `ref_1` is the first clip's frame 0
in every clip after it. A ref that is empty in a clip (clip 1, before anything
was remembered) is `None` for Reference to Video, which skips it, and blocked
for any other node; to look at a clip's references, wire Orrery Refs' `preview`
output (all of them as one batch) into a Preview Image. Reference to Video
reads the first image of each reference only: several stills go to several
images, and a batch is for nodes that take many, such as Create H3 RefMod From
Inputs. Frames past a clip's end are left out (the last frame stands in when
none is left). Fetched frames are kept as anchors; Orrery Refs' `keep_sent`
holds them from clip 1 in the next run, so a character can carry over from one
reel to another. A walk-through:
[Orrery Refs, lesson 3](orrery-refs.md#6-lesson-3-anchors-across-a-reel-with-remember).

### 1e. RefMods (`refmod NAME`, `SET: @JINX(0.5, 35%)`)

A RefMod, from the [ComfyUI-H3RefMods](https://github.com/FranckyB/ComfyUI-H3RefMods)
pack, is a reference H3 takes as extra tokens. It is built once from stills of a
person or a place and brings them back in a later clip. One ships with orrery,
in `examples/refmods`: `minimaxh3_jinx_v1_refmod`, Jinx from League of Legends
(Apache 2.0, from [malcolmrey/minimaxh3](https://huggingface.co/malcolmrey/minimaxh3)).
Orrery RefMods finds it there; RefMods of your own go into `models/refmods`.
A CAST member names it:

```
CAST
@JINX (refmod minimaxh3_jinx_v1_refmod): a young woman
SCENE
SHOT 5s: static
@JINX waves and walks out of the frame.
SCENE
SHOT 5s: static
The empty room.
SCENE
SHOT 5s: static
@JINX slides back into the frame.
```

The second clip never names JINX, so it leaves her out: her definition, her
images and her RefMod. The third names her again, and the RefMod brings her back.
The CAST does not describe her: her look comes from the RefMod. The example
workflow `orrery_h3_reel_refmod.json` plays this reel.

**The dials: `SET:`.** A member's name turns all of her pictures and RefMods;
a word in the parentheses chooses among them, in the CAST's own words; the
numbers are (strength), (strength, start) or (strength, start, end):

```
SET: @JINX(0.6)                        all of Jinx's references
SET: @JINX(0.6, refmods)               all her RefMods
SET: @JINX(0.6, images)                all her pictures, remembered ones included
SET: @JINX(0.3, image 1)               just that picture
SET: @JINX(1, 35%, refmod jinx_today)  just that RefMod, from 35%
SET: image_1(0.5, 35%), minimaxh3_jinx_v1_refmod(0.3)    by a picture's or a RefMod's own name
SET: refmods(1, 35%)                   the RefMods without dials of their own
```

In the head a SET line holds for every clip, in a scene for that clip; the later
line wins (between a member and her references too), and a word that matches
none of her references is lint. A picture's own name dials any image the clip
hands to H3, a CAST source, a frame anchor or one named in the text (`[image 1]`,
`<Image 1>`), and a RefMod's own name brings that RefMod into the clip when no
member has it: a reference needs no CAST (tutorial 19).

**One way to turn the model's knobs (#227).** A LoRA, a RefMod, a picture and a
member all change how the model behaves, and SET takes them all, LoRAs
included, with the same (strength, start, end). A short name is told by its
file; the long form says its kind and never collides:

```
SET: turbo(0.8, 0%, 50%)                         a LoRA, for the first half of sampling
SET: <lora:turbo:0.8, 0%, 50%>                   the same, its kind written out
SET: <refmod:jinx_v1:1, 0%, 10%>, <image:1:0.6>, <cast:JINX:0.6, refmods>
SET: image_1(0.3|0.6|1)                          a sweep: Roll renders all three
```

A name that is a LoRA and a RefMod too is lint, and asks for the long form. A
LoRA's start and end go on by ComfyUI's hook keyframes: it comes on at its
start and goes off at its end (at 4 turbo steps, as coarse as a RefMod's start).
`LORA:` lines take the same tags, as before.

| Dial | Meaning |
|---|---|
| strength | how firmly it holds: 1 as built, lower looser (the prompt's description has more say), 0 leaves it out |
| start, `35%` | it waits for that share of sampling, while H3 lays out the picture. A place's RefMod then drags less of the framing it was built from along; a character's identity is laid out in those first steps too, so it needs them. The fewer the steps, the coarser the dial: with a 4-step LoRA the first step lays out nearly everything, and a later start can miss a character entirely. 0% unless said otherwise |
| end, `10%` | it stops at that share of sampling: `(1, 0%, 10%)` lays a character out and then lets the prompt take over. 100% unless said otherwise |
| a picture's strength | by default 1, from 0%, to 100%. At 0 it leaves the clip, as in a scene without it: no `<Picture N>`, and Orrery Refs does not hand it on. Between 0 and 1 the strength reaches both ways the picture comes in: its reference latents, and the vision tokens its text encoder left in the text. Most of the change comes below 0.3: Jinx's picture is gone at 0.01, half there at 0.1 (her face and braid, not yet her dress), and nearly all there at 0.2. Before its start and after its end the picture is held at 0 the same way. Only the prompt, as the text encoder read it after the picture, keeps what it saw, and that is a styling hint at most, not a face or an outfit |
| `always` | in the CAST's parentheses: the member goes with every clip, named or not |

The CAST's own `refmod NAME at 0.5 from 35% to 80%`, `image 1 at 0.5` and a
`refmods: at … from …` line set the same dials, as earlier orrery wrote them;
SET wins over them.

**From the reel itself:** `REMEMBER: every 10th frame as refmod jinx_today` in a scene turns frames of its clip into a RefMod, which the clips after it get wherever the CAST names `refmod jinx_today` (her look of today, outfit included). Before the remembering scene has played, a clip goes without it. Orrery RefMods encodes the frames with the H3 video VAE wired into its `vae` (at most 73 of them, see [comfyui.md](comfyui.md)); no file and no pack are involved.

**Per scene:** a scene's own CAST replaces a member for that clip, in its place
(so it keeps its `<Subject N>`), with all its sources and dials:

```
SCENE the return
CAST
@JINX (refmod minimaxh3_jinx_v1_refmod, image 1): a young woman
SET: @JINX(0.3, refmods), @JINX(0.5, images)
```

**Which clips get a member:** every clip leaves out the CAST members it does not
name (in its prose, a voice, an SFX line, a frame anchor or the summary), unless
they are `always`. A defined subject that the screenplay never calls for still
shows up in H3's clip. In a clip on its own, lint says so.

**How the strength works:** H3 has no strength for references, and the pack's own
strength only blurs one. When its nodes load, orrery wraps H3's attention in
memory (ComfyUI's files stay as they are) and gives a RefMod about `s` times its
share of the attention. At 1, H3 runs as it would without orrery. Number ranges
work as everywhere: `SET: @JINX({0.4-0.8})` rolls and records it. **Orrery
RefMods** puts the RefMods on the conditioning ([comfyui.md](comfyui.md));
without it, lint says so. The experiment behind the start:
[long-video.md](long-video.md#the-experiment-before-stages-4-and-5).

### 2. Middle: the scene model

`{mode, ratio, style, shots[{duration, transition, camera, prose, voices[], sfx[]}], music, speakers}`.
The picks live here, so gallery ratings are about scenes, not about one
target's serialization: a loved "frozen × lynx" stays loved whether rendered
as a Krea2 still or an H3 video.

### 3. Writers

- **`h3-base`**: computes every mechanical invariant deterministically:
  shot numbering and `At MM:SS.mmm` from cumulative durations; the alignment
  instruction per mode, byte-exact including the official strings'
  inconsistent brackets (FL2VA writes `Picture 1 (from Shot 1)`, L2VA writes
  `<Picture 1> (from [Shot N])`); speaker IDs by first vocal event; `<d>`
  wrapping and language tags; the voiceover phrase and lip clause; camera
  sentences ("push in, small, slow" → "The camera pushes in with small
  amplitude at slow speed."); the soundscape paragraph from `SFX` lines;
  `N/A` rules.
- **`h3-ref`**: the six sections of the full-reference format, every label (`<Subject N>`,
  `<Picture i>`, `<Video k>`, `<Audio j>`, `(Sx)`) computed from the CAST (1b).
- **`flat`**: one natural-language string for image models (Krea2, Flux,
  H3 stills), dropping audio and later shots.

### 4. Lint

Checked against the guides; most rules cannot be violated because the writer
computes them. What remains:

- duration 4–15 s;
- keyframe modes: `<Picture 1>` anchored in the first shot (I2VA), both
  endpoints anchored (FL2VA), the last frame anchored in the final shot
  (L2VA); FL2VA with several shots gets a warning (the guide favors one);
- frames wired into the Orrery Prompt (`first_frame`, `last_frame`): the clip
  takes their shape, an info note when the header's ratio is set aside, a
  warning when the two frames differ in shape (H3 crops the last) or a written
  `: w… h…` does not match (H3 stretches the first);
- mood words in `MUSIC` (use instrumentation, tempo, dynamics);
- missing `SFX` (an info note: the soundscape then asks for natural foley and
  ambience; `N/A` only for explicit silence);
- unknown camera motion (with the vocabulary as suggestion);
- CLIP weight syntax (inert on H3);
- unresolved `__lib__` references (hand off to the wildcard manager).

### 5. Optional densifier

The one job Context-IR does that a compiler cannot: "supplement missing or
underspecified semantic details". orrery offers it per field: a configurable
model (local Qwen by default) gets the official guide as context and may
enrich **prose only**: shot action text and soundscape sentences. It never
touches structure, timestamps, IDs or tags, and its output is logged like a
`>` enhancement pick. The writer re-validates afterwards.

## Built-in libraries for H3

Shipped with orrery, derived from the official vocabulary:

- `__camera__`: motions with sensible amplitude/speed combinations.
- `__h3style__`: the guide's styles (live-action, cinematic; 2D-animated;
  3D CG; claymation; watercolor; vintage film).
- `__cut__`: the guide's cut verbs.

## LoRAs and dials

Pyro's captioning rule from the H3 campaign: caption every property that
should stay a prompt-controllable dial; omit intrinsic subject properties that
should be absorbed as identity. For orrery that means: with a LoRA loaded,
libraries should vary the captioned dials (setting, light, camera, action),
never the absorbed identity.

## Prior art

- Official `h3-prompt-writing` skill: LLM rewrite following the guide.
  orrery's densifier uses the same guide, but only for prose.
- [Fantastic H3 Prompt Builder](https://github.com/Adudeguyman/ComfyUI-Fantastic-MiniMaxH3-PromptBuilder)
  (ComfyUI): hand-filled templates per mode, live
  rule checking, media loader, RefMods. orrery does not duplicate media
  handling; its compiled text can feed that workflow. RefMods load through the
  H3RefMods pack (Orrery RefMods, 1e).
